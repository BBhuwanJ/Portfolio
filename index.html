Research

My research interests are in retrieval-augmented generation, knowledge-grounded reasoning, low-resource NLP, legal AI, and reliable evaluation of AI systems. I am especially interested in treating RAG as a retrieve, reason, generate, and verify process instead of a simple retrieve-then-generate pipeline.

In my current work, I compare passage construction and retrieval strategies for grounded question answering over Nepali statutory text. I study how retrieval failures, missing evidence, unsupported reasoning, and generation errors can be separated through component-wise evaluation.

Personal

My academic interests come from a simple habit: I like understanding why systems work, where they fail, and how they can be made more reliable. I am drawn to research that connects careful problem formulation with practical implementation, especially when the work can support people in learning, decision-making, or access to information.

I enjoy turning unclear problems into structured workflows, experiments, and software systems that can be tested and improved. Across my research, product, and community work, I value clarity, reproducibility, disciplined evaluation, and the patience to inspect failures instead of hiding them.

Long term, I want to contribute to technology that is useful, trustworthy, and grounded in real human needs. I am especially motivated by environments where research, software engineering, mentorship, and service can reinforce each other.
